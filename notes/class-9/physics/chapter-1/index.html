Class 9 Physics Chapter 1 Notes — Physical Quantities and Measurement NBF Syllabus

Chapter Overview: Chapter 1 of Class 9 Physics (NBF Syllabus) introduces students to the fundamental concepts of physical quantities and their measurement. This chapter covers base and derived quantities, the SI system of units, scientific notation, significant figures, measuring instruments like Vernier calipers and screw gauge, and the concepts of error, precision, and accuracy. These concepts form the foundation of all physics studied in subsequent chapters and are essential for board exams.

  • SI units — base units and derived units
  • Scientific notation and prefixes (mega, kilo, milli, micro, nano, pico)
  • Scalars and vectors
  • Significant figures and rules
  • Vernier calipers: construction and working
  • Screw gauge: pitch, least count, and usage
  • Measuring cylinder for volume measurement
  • Random and systematic errors
  • Precision vs. accuracy

Short Questions and Answers

Q1. How does physics play an important role in our life?

Physics plays an important role in our daily lives by helping us understand how things work around us. It explains objects' movement, energy flow, and how forces like gravity affect us. From the technology we use, like smartphones and computers, to transportation, medicine, and even cooking, physics is involved in making these things possible.

For example, the cars we drive work because of principles of motion, and we use electricity (which is governed by physics) to power our homes. In simple terms, physics helps us understand the world and makes our lives easier and more efficient.

Q2. Estimate your age in minutes and seconds.

To estimate your age in minutes and seconds (for a 16-year-old student):

  • Minutes in one year = 365 × 24 × 60 = 525,600 minutes
  • Age in minutes = 16 × 525,600 = 8,409,600 minutes
  • Age in seconds = 8,409,600 × 60 = 504,576,000 seconds

So a 16-year-old's age is approximately 8,409,600 minutes or 504,576,000 seconds.

Q3. What base quantities are involved in force, pressure, power, and charge?

  • Force (F = ma): Mass (kg), Length (m), Time (s)
  • Pressure (P = F/A): Mass (kg), Length (m), Time (s)
  • Power (P = W/t): Mass (kg), Length (m), Time (s)
  • Charge (Q = It): Electric current (A) and Time (s)

Q4. Show that the prefix "micro" is a thousand times smaller than "milli."

The prefix "micro" represents 10⁻⁶ and "milli" represents 10⁻³.

Dividing: 10⁻⁶ ÷ 10⁻³ = 10⁻⁶⁺³ = 10⁻³ = 1/1000

This shows that micro is indeed 1000 times smaller than milli.

Q5. Why is displacement a vector quantity while energy is a scalar quantity?

Displacement is a vector quantity because it includes both distance and direction. For example, walking 5 km requires specifying the direction (like north or south). In contrast, energy is a scalar quantity, needing only a numerical value for description. Saying "I have 100 joules of energy" is sufficient since direction is not needed. That's why displacement is a vector and energy is a scalar.

Q6. Why can a screw gauge give more precise measurements than Vernier calipers?

A screw gauge provides more precise measurements than Vernier calipers because it has a smaller least count.

  • Least count of screw gauge = 0.01 mm (or 0.001 cm)
  • Least count of Vernier calipers = 0.1 mm (or 0.01 cm)

The screw mechanism allows for very small movements, making it more precise. Thus, screw gauges are better for measurements requiring high precision.

Q7. Differentiate between a mechanical stopwatch and a digital stopwatch.

Mechanical Stopwatch:

  • Works using a mechanical spring system
  • Time is displayed using rotating hands on a dial
  • Requires manual winding to operate
  • Less accurate compared to digital stopwatches

Digital Stopwatch:

  • Works using an electronic system
  • Time is displayed on a digital screen in numbers
  • Powered by batteries
  • More accurate and easier to use

Q8. How is a measuring cylinder used to measure the volume of an irregularly shaped stone?

  1. Fill the measuring cylinder with water up to a certain level and note the initial water level.
  2. Submerge the irregularly shaped stone completely in the water.
  3. The water level will rise — note the new water level.
  4. Subtract the initial water level from the new water level.

The difference in water levels gives the volume of the stone, which equals the volume of water displaced.

Q9. What precautions should be considered while using a measuring cylinder?

  1. Ensure the cylinder is clean and dry before use.
  2. Place the cylinder on a stable and level surface.
  3. Fill the cylinder slowly and carefully to avoid splashing.
  4. Record the initial water level accurately.
  5. Avoid parallax error by reading the water level (meniscus) at eye level.
  6. Take multiple readings for accuracy.

Q10. Why do we need to consider significant digits in measurements?

Significant digits are important because they show the precision of a measurement and indicate the level of uncertainty involved. They help ensure that measurements are accurate and reliable. By using significant digits:

  1. We avoid errors in calculations.
  2. We prevent misleading results by not overstating precision.
  3. We maintain honesty and transparency in scientific communication.

Q11. How can random errors be reduced?

Random errors can be reduced by:

  1. Increase sample size: Take more measurements to average out random fluctuations.
  2. Improve instrument precision: Use high-quality, calibrated instruments.
  3. Control environmental factors: Regulate temperature, humidity, and other conditions.
  4. Take multiple measurements: Average multiple readings to reduce impact of individual errors.
  5. Calibrate instruments: Regularly ensure instruments are accurate.

Q12. Differentiate between precision and accuracy.

Precision: Refers to how closely repeated measurements are to each other. It shows the consistency of results. Example: Measuring the same value multiple times and getting similar results shows precision.

Accuracy: Refers to how close a measurement is to the actual (true) value. It shows the correctness of the results. Example: A measurement that matches the true value is accurate.

In summary, precision is about consistency, while accuracy is about correctness. Both are important for reliable measurements.

Long Response Questions and Answers

Q1. Define Physics. Describe its revolutionary role in technology.

Definition of Physics: Physics is a fundamental branch of science concerned with understanding the nature and properties of matter, energy, space, and time. It is the study of how the physical universe operates at both macroscopic and subatomic scales.

Revolutionary Role in Technology:

  • Information & Digital Technology: The exploration of solid-state physics, quantum mechanics, and semiconductors directly enabled the invention of the transistor — the basis for modern microchips, computers, smartphones, and global telecommunications.
  • Transportation & Aerospace: The laws of classical mechanics and thermodynamics enabled steam engines, internal combustion engines, and jet propulsion systems. Electromagnetic principles paved the way for high-speed Maglev trains.
  • Medical Advancements: MRI, CT scans, X-rays, and diagnostic ultrasounds are direct applications of nuclear physics, electromagnetism, and optics.
  • Energy Infrastructure: Nuclear power plants, photovoltaic solar setups, and hydrodynamic wind turbines are all driven by discoveries in physics.

Q2. List with brief description the different branches of physics.

  • Mechanics: The study of motion of physical bodies, its underlying causes (forces), and its systematic effects. Broadly divided into kinematics and dynamics.
  • Thermodynamics: The study of heat, temperature, and their dynamic relationships to work, energy, and the physical properties of matter.
  • Electricity and Magnetism: The study of electric charges at rest (electrostatics) and in motion (electrodynamics), along with their interactions with magnetic fields.
  • Atomic Physics: Investigates the internal structure, arrangement, energy states, and behavioral characteristics of individual isolated atoms.
  • Nuclear Physics: Focused on the constituents, structural properties, interactions, and behavioral dynamics of the atomic nucleus.
  • Quantum Mechanics: Deals with the mathematical description and physical behavior of matter and light at microscopic, atomic, and subatomic scales.
  • Astrophysics: Application of fundamental physical laws to explain the behavior, composition, lifecycle, and evolution of celestial objects.
  • Plasma Physics: The study of the production, properties, and behavior of highly ionized matter known as plasma — often referred to as the fourth state of matter.

Q3. What are physical quantities? Distinguish between base and derived physical quantities. Give at least three examples.

Physical Quantities: A physical quantity is any measurable attribute or characteristic of a material, object, or physical system that can be quantified numerically alongside a standardized unit of measurement.

FeatureBase Physical QuantitiesDerived Physical Quantities
DefinitionFoundational quantities in terms of which others are formally defined.Quantities whose definitions are built from combinations of base quantities.
IndependenceCompletely independent; cannot be reduced further.Dependent directly upon base quantities.
SI FrameworkThere are exactly 7 defined base quantities.An extensive, open-ended number exist.
ExamplesLength, Mass, Time, Electric Current, TemperatureSpeed, Acceleration, Force, Work, Power, Density

Verification:

  • Speed (v) = Distance / Time = Length (Base) / Time (Base)
  • Area (A) = Length × Width = Length² (Base)
  • Density (ρ) = Mass / Volume = Mass (Base) / Length³ (Base)

Q4. What do you mean by unit of a physical quantity? Define base units and derived units.

Unit of a Physical Quantity: A unit is a universally accepted, standardized reference magnitude chosen to measure and express a specific physical quantity. Any complete measurement consists of both a numerical value and its corresponding unit designation.

Base Units: Standard reference measures explicitly assigned to the 7 base physical quantities within the SI framework. They cannot be derived from other units. They are: meter (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole (mol), and candela (cd).

Derived Units: Units of measurement obtained by algebraic combinations of one or more base units via multiplication or division. For instance, velocity is meters per second (m/s), and force is the Newton (1 N = 1 kg·m/s²).

Q5. What are prefixes? What is their use in measurements?

Prefixes: In the SI system, prefixes are standardized words or letters added immediately before a unit to signify decimal multiples or sub-multiples of that unit in fixed powers of 10 (e.g., kilo denotes 10³, micro denotes 10⁻⁶).

Use in Measurements: Prefixes simplify reporting and calculations by eliminating long strings of zeros from extremely large or small numbers:

  • Instead of writing 0.0000005 meters, we write 5 μm (micrometers).
  • Instead of expressing 4,500,000 Watts, we write 4.5 MW (Megawatts).

Q6. What is scientific notation? Give at least five examples.

Scientific Notation: Scientific notation (or standard form) is a mathematical layout used to write numbers that are either extremely large or extremely small. It expresses values as a product of a decimal multiplier between 1 and 10 and a power of 10.

Formula: M × 10ⁿ (where 1 ≤ M < 10 and n is any integer)

Five Examples:

  • Mean orbital distance Earth to Moon: 384,000,000 m = 3.84 × 10⁸ m
  • Rest mass of an electron: 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg
  • Speed of light in vacuum: 300,000,000 m/s = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s
  • Average diameter of a plant cell: 0.00003 m = 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ m
  • Equatorial diameter of Earth: 12,742,000 m = 1.2742 × 10⁷ m

Q7. Describe construction and working of Vernier calipers in detail.

Construction: A standard Vernier caliper consists of a Main Scale (a fixed metallic track graduated in millimeters and centimeters) and a Vernier Scale (a specialized slide block containing incremental divisions that slides past the main scale). It features Lower Jaws for outside dimensions, Upper Jaws for internal dimensions, and a Depth Probe at the rear tail to gauge hole depths.

Working Procedure:

  1. Bring the jaws to a closed state to determine any Zero Error.
  2. Place the target object securely between the respective measurement jaws.
  3. Note the Main Scale Reading (MSR) — the final mark passed by the Vernier zero line.
  4. Find the precise division line on the Vernier scale that forms a perfectly straight line with any main scale line to get the Vernier Scale Reading (VSR).
  5. Total Measured Length = MSR + (VSR × Least Count) ± Zero Correction

Q8. What is screw gauge? What is its pitch and least count? How is it used to measure thickness of thin copper wire?

Screw Gauge: A screw gauge (micrometer) is a highly precise measurement instrument designed to gauge very small dimensions, such as wire diameters or sheet thicknesses, typically yielding an exceptional precision threshold up to 0.01 mm.

Pitch and Least Count: Pitch is the linear distance advanced along the main axis scale by the movable spindle when the circular thimble completes one single full (360°) rotation. Least Count (LC) = Pitch ÷ Total Divisions on Circular Scale.

Measuring Copper Wire Thickness:

  1. Check for any initial zero error by closing the spindle completely against the anvil.
  2. Retract the spindle, insert the thin copper wire into the open void, and turn the thimble until the wire is lightly gripped.
  3. Advance using only the rear ratchet until it produces consecutive clicks.
  4. Record the Main Scale Reading (MSR) and the Circular Scale Reading (CSR).
  5. Total wire thickness = MSR + (CSR × LC) ± Zero Correction

Q9. Define error. Differentiate between random and systematic error. How can these errors be reduced?

Error: An error represents the algebraic difference between the experimentally observed measurement value of a physical quantity and its accepted, true theoretical standard value.

FeatureRandom ErrorSystematic Error
CauseUnpredictable environmental shifts or micro-variations in observer focus.Faulty tool calibration, zero offsets, or systematic design flaws.
DirectionInconsistent; values fluctuate irregularly in both positive and negative directions.Highly consistent; always shifts values in a single direction.
RepeatabilityRepetitive tests yield varying decimal numbers.Repetitive tests yield identical, off-center values.

Methods to Reduce Errors:

  • Random Errors: Can be minimized by taking an extensive series of independent measurements and computing their arithmetic mean (average).
  • Systematic Errors: Are eliminated by checking and zero-correcting instruments prior to use and cross-calibrating diagnostic gear against pristine laboratory standards.

Q10. Differentiate between scalars and vectors. Justify individual allocations.

Scalars vs. Vectors: Scalar quantities are physical metrics that are comprehensively defined by their magnitude alone without reference to direction. Vector quantities require a declaration of both absolute magnitude and a specific spatial direction to be completely defined.

Justification for Scalars:

  • Distance: Represents the continuous integrated length of a path traversed; it accumulates positively regardless of turns.
  • Speed: Measures the scalar rate of distance traveled over time; remains identical whether heading North or spinning in a circle.
  • Mass: A fundamental measure of matter content possessing zero geographic directionality.
  • Energy: Represents the basic capacity of a system to perform work; an absolute net scalar value lacking a directional arrow.

Justification for Vectors:

  • Displacement: Represents the straight-line spatial separation vector extending from a fixed starting point to an ending position; requires a directional heading.
  • Velocity: Defined as the time rate of change of displacement, bridging speed with direction.
  • Acceleration: The mathematical rate at which a velocity vector changes over time, acting either along or opposite to the path of motion.
  • Force: An action that causes mass to accelerate; its direction determines the direction of the resulting acceleration vector.

Q12. Differentiate between precision and accuracy (Long Answer).

Precision vs. Accuracy: Precision refers to the degree of consistency, agreement, or repeatability across a series of independent measurements taken under identical conditions. Accuracy refers to how closely an experimentally observed value matches the actual, true, globally accepted standard value of that physical quantity.

Comparative MetricPrecisionAccuracy
Primary FocusConsistency, reproducibility, and the tightness of clustered data points.Closeness to the actual, targeted, true absolute value.
Instrument FactorDirectly governed by the instrument's fine resolution or fractional Least Count.Directly governed by proper tool calibration and minimization of systematic errors.

Q3. A boy has an age of 15 years, 2 months, and 10 days. Convert his age into (a) Seconds, (b) Milliseconds, (c) Megaseconds.

Total Days = (15 × 365) + (2 × 30) + 10 = 5475 + 60 + 10 = 5545 days

(a) Seconds = 5545 × 24 × 60 × 60 = 5545 × 86400 = 479,088,000 s
(b) Milliseconds = 479,088,000 × 10³ = 4.79088 × 10¹¹ ms
(c) Megaseconds = 479,088,000 / 10⁶ = 479.088 Ms

Q4. How many kilometers are there in 25 micrometers?

1 μm = 10⁻⁶ m; 1 m = 10⁻³ km
25 μm = 25 × 10⁻⁶ m = 25 × 10⁻⁶ × 10⁻³ km
= 2.5 × 10⁻⁸ km

Q5. What is the pitch and least count of: (a) Vernier Calipers, (b) Screw Gauge

(a) Vernier Calipers:
Given: Smallest division on main scale = 1 mm; Total divisions on Vernier scale = 20
Least Count = 1 mm / 20 = 0.05 mm
Pitch (Value of 1 Main Scale Division) = 1 mm

(b) Screw Gauge:
Given: Pitch = 0.5 mm; Divisions on circular scale = 50
Least Count = 0.5 mm / 50 = 0.01 mm

Q6. Vernier Calipers Reading Analysis: MSR = 2.3 cm, Coinciding Vernier Division = 6

LC = 0.01 cm
Total Reading = MSR + (Coinciding Division × LC)
= 2.3 cm + (6 × 0.01 cm)
= 2.3 + 0.06
Total Reading = 2.36 cm

Q7. Screw Gauge Reading: MSR = 5.5 mm, Coinciding Circular Scale Division = 45

LC = 0.01 mm
Total Diameter = MSR + (CSR × LC)
= 5.5 mm + (45 × 0.01 mm)
= 5.5 + 0.45
Total Diameter of Ball = 5.95 mm
