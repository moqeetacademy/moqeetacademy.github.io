Class 9 Physics Chapter 6 Notes — Work, Energy and Power NBF Syllabus

Chapter Overview: Chapter 6 of Class 9 Physics (NBF Syllabus) covers Work, Energy and Power — one of the most important chapters for board exams. Students learn how to calculate work done using the formula W = Fd cosθ, understand kinetic and potential energy with their derivations, apply the law of conservation of energy, calculate power and efficiency, and distinguish between renewable and non-renewable energy sources. Energy conversion chains and energy flow diagrams are also key parts of this chapter. These topics are heavily tested in both short questions and numericals in FBISE and Punjab Board exams.

Key Topics in This Chapter

  • Definition of work and its SI unit (Joule)
  • Formula W = Fd cosθ — conditions for maximum and minimum work
  • Kinetic energy and its derivation — K.E. = ½mv²
  • Gravitational potential energy — P.E. = mgh
  • Law of conservation of energy
  • Energy conversion — examples from daily life
  • Power — definition, formula P = W/t, SI unit (Watt)
  • Horsepower — relation 1 hp = 746 W
  • Efficiency — formula and why it can never be 100%
  • Renewable vs non-renewable energy sources
  • Energy flow diagrams (steady state systems)
  • Electricity generation from fossil fuels — block diagram

Short Questions

Q1. A car is moving with a constant speed along a straight road. Is there any work done on the car?

No, the net work done on the car is zero because its speed remains constant. Since there is no change in kinetic energy, no net work is done.

Q2. Does the work done in raising a box up in a building depend upon how fast it is raised up? Through which path? To how much height?

No, the work done does not depend on speed or path. It only depends on the weight of the box and the height to which it is raised.

Q3. Work done on the body either speeds it up or slows it down. Keeping it in mind, explain how much work is done by centripetal force on an orbiting satellite?

The work done by centripetal force is zero because the force acts perpendicular to the direction of motion of the satellite, so it only changes direction, not speed.

Q4. A car has kinetic energy Eₖ. By what factor will its kinetic energy change if its velocity is doubled?

The kinetic energy becomes four times because kinetic energy depends on the square of velocity. If velocity doubles, energy increases by 2² = 4.

Q5. A bullet is fired from a gun, bullet penetrates into sand wall and it stops. Where is its kinetic energy used?

The bullet's kinetic energy is converted into heat, sound, and the work done in penetrating the sand wall.

Q6. An LED light bulb has efficiency of 80%. Does it violate conservation of energy principle?

No, it does not violate the conservation of energy principle because the remaining 20% of energy is lost mostly as heat. Total energy in = total energy out.

Q7. How does using renewable energy sources contribute to reducing environmental impact compared to non-renewable sources?

Renewable energy sources produce less pollution and fewer harmful gases. This helps reduce environmental damage and protects natural resources for future generations.

Q8. Will we eventually rely entirely on renewable energy sources? Why or why not?

It may happen in the future, but currently it is difficult because renewable sources depend on weather conditions and require better energy storage systems to be reliable at all times.

Q9. How can increasing the power of a machine impact its energy consumption?

Increasing the power of a machine usually increases its energy consumption because the machine uses more energy in a shorter time (P = W/t).

Q10. A perpetual engine has an efficiency equal to 1. Why will it not work?

A perpetual engine cannot work because some energy is always lost as heat due to friction and other factors, so 100% efficiency is impossible in practice.

Long Questions

Q1. Define work and its unit. Describe the conditions for maximum and minimum work.

Definition of Work: Work is said to be done when a force acts on a body and moves it through a certain distance in the direction of the force.

The work done is given by:

W = F × d × cosθ

Where: W = Work done, F = Force applied, d = Displacement, θ = Angle between force and displacement.

SI Unit of Work: The SI unit of work is Joule (J). One joule is the work done when a force of one newton moves a body through one meter.
1 J = 1 N × 1 m

Conditions for Maximum Work: Work is maximum when force and displacement are in the same direction. In this case θ = 0°, so cos 0° = 1, therefore W = Fd (maximum).

Conditions for Minimum Work: Work is minimum (zero) when force acts perpendicular to displacement. In this case θ = 90°, so cos 90° = 0, therefore W = 0.

Q2. What is kinetic energy? Derive its expression by using graphical analysis.

Kinetic Energy: The energy possessed by a body due to its motion is called kinetic energy. If a body of mass m moves with velocity v, its kinetic energy is: K.E. = ½mv²

Derivation by Graphical Analysis: Consider a body of mass m moving with initial velocity u. A constant force F acts on it and increases its velocity to v in time t.

Using the velocity-time graph, the distance covered equals the area under the graph (trapezium). Starting from rest (u = 0) and using the work-energy theorem:

Work done = Force × distance = ma × d
Using v² = u² + 2ad → d = v²/2a
W = ma × v²/2a = ½mv²
∴ K.E. = ½mv²

Q3. What is potential energy? What are its different types? Show that gravitational potential energy = mgh.

Potential Energy: The energy possessed by a body due to its position or shape is called potential energy.

Types of Potential Energy:

  • Gravitational Potential Energy — due to height above ground
  • Elastic Potential Energy — due to compression or stretching (e.g., spring)
  • Chemical Potential Energy — stored in chemical bonds (e.g., food, fuel)

Proof that Gravitational P.E. = mgh: When a body of mass m is lifted to height h, the work done against gravity equals the potential energy stored.

W = F × d = (mg) × h
∴ P.E. = mgh (Hence proved)

Q4. What is meant by energy conversion and energy conservation?

Energy Conversion: The process of changing energy from one form into another form is called energy conversion.

Examples of Energy Conversion:

  • Electric bulb: Electrical energy → Light and heat energy
  • Fan: Electrical energy → Mechanical energy
  • Battery: Chemical energy → Electrical energy
Law of Conservation of Energy: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be converted from one form to another. The total amount of energy in the universe always remains constant.

Q5. Describe how useful energy may be obtained from natural resources.

Useful energy can be obtained from natural resources by converting their stored energy into heat or electricity.

  • Solar Energy: Solar panels convert sunlight into electricity.
  • Wind Energy: Wind turbines convert wind energy into electrical energy.
  • Hydroelectric Energy: Flowing water rotates turbines to generate electricity.
  • Fossil Fuels: Coal, oil, and gas are burned to produce heat energy.
  • Biomass: Wood and plant waste are burned to produce useful energy.

These energy resources are used in homes, industries, and transportation across Pakistan and worldwide.

Q7. Describe the processes by which energy is converted from one form to another.

  • Fossil Fuel: Chemical Energy → Heat Energy → Mechanical Energy → Electrical Energy
  • Hydroelectric: Potential Energy of Water → Kinetic Energy → Mechanical Energy → Electrical Energy
  • Solar: Light Energy → Electrical Energy
  • Nuclear: Nuclear Energy → Heat Energy → Mechanical Energy → Electrical Energy
  • Geothermal: Heat Energy from Earth → Mechanical Energy → Electrical Energy
  • Wind: Kinetic Energy of Wind → Mechanical Energy → Electrical Energy
  • Biomass: Chemical Energy → Heat Energy → Electrical Energy

Q8. Describe the process of electricity generation from fossil fuel input to electricity output.

Fossil Fuel → Boiler → Steam → Turbine → Generator → Electricity

Explanation:

  • Fossil fuel is burned in the boiler.
  • Heat converts water into steam.
  • Steam rotates the turbine.
  • Turbine drives the generator.
  • Generator produces electricity.

Q9. Define power. What is the relation of its SI unit with horse power?

Power: Power is the rate of doing work. P = W / t
Where: P = Power, W = Work done, t = Time
SI Unit of Power: The SI unit of power is Watt (W). One watt means one joule of work done in one second. 1 W = 1 J/s

Relation Between Watt and Horsepower:

1 hp = 746 W

Thus, one horsepower is equal to 746 watts.

Q10. What is efficiency? Why is it important for cars or electronic devices to be designed with high efficiency? Why can efficiency never be 100%?

Efficiency: Efficiency is the ratio of useful output energy to input energy.
Efficiency = (Useful Output / Input) × 100%

Importance of High Efficiency:

  • Saves fuel and electricity
  • Reduces energy wastage
  • Lowers operating cost
  • Produces less pollution

Why Efficiency Cannot be 100%: Efficiency can never be 100% because some energy is always lost as heat, sound, or due to friction. Therefore, no machine can convert all input energy into useful output energy.

Q11. Explain energy flow diagrams through steady state systems.

1. Filament Lamp: Electrical Energy → Light Energy + Heat Energy. A filament lamp converts electrical energy into light, but some energy is wasted as heat.

2. Power Station: Chemical Energy → Heat Energy → Mechanical Energy → Electrical Energy. Fuel burns to produce heat, which produces steam. Steam rotates turbines connected to generators.

3. Vehicle Traveling at Constant Speed: Chemical Energy → Mechanical Energy → Kinetic Energy + Heat + Sound. The engine converts fuel energy into motion while some energy is lost due to friction and sound.

Numerical Problems with Solutions

Q1. Calculate the work done in pushing a box with a force of 150 N through a distance of 5 m.

Given: Force (F) = 150 N, Distance (d) = 5 m

Formula: W = F × d
W = 150 × 5
W = 750 J
✅ Final Answer: 750 J

Q2. A boy weighing 75 N jumps up and gains 300 J of gravitational potential energy. Find the height to which the boy will rise.

Given: P.E. = 300 J, Weight (w = mg) = 75 N

Formula: P.E. = Weight × h
300 = 75 × h
h = 300 / 75
h = 4 m
✅ Final Answer: 4 m

Q3. A 5 kg steel ball is dropped from the top of a 12 m high tower. What is the kinetic energy of the ball on hitting the ground? With what velocity will it hit the ground?

Given: m = 5 kg, h = 12 m, g = 9.8 m/s²

Part (a) — Kinetic Energy: By conservation of energy, K.E. = P.E. lost
K.E. = m × g × h = 5 × 9.8 × 12 = 588 J

Part (b) — Velocity: K.E. = ½mv²
588 = ½ × 5 × v²
v² = 588 / 2.5 = 235.2
v = √235.2 = 15.3 m/s
✅ K.E. = 588 J  |  Velocity = 15.3 m/s

Q4. An object of mass 2.0 kg is dropped from a height of 20 m. Find: (a) its kinetic energy after falling 15 m, and (b) its gravitational potential energy when it has fallen 15 m.

Given: m = 2.0 kg, total height = 20 m, distance fallen = 15 m, remaining height = 5 m, g = 9.8 m/s²

Part (a) — K.E. after falling 15 m:
K.E. = m × g × h_fallen = 2 × 9.8 × 15 = 294 J

Part (b) — P.E. at remaining height (5 m):
P.E. = m × g × h_remaining = 2 × 9.8 × 5 = 98 J
✅ K.E. = 294 J  |  P.E. = 98 J

Q5. A rocket with a mass of 800 g is launched vertically upward with an initial speed of 30 m/s. (a) Calculate maximum theoretical height. (b) If it actually reaches 25 m, find work done against air resistance.

Given: m = 800 g = 0.8 kg, v = 30 m/s, g = 9.8 m/s², actual height = 25 m

Part (a) — Maximum theoretical height:
Using energy conservation: ½mv² = mgh → h = v²/2g
h = (30)² / (2 × 9.8) = 900 / 19.6 = 45.92 m

Part (b) — Work done against air resistance:
K.E. initial = ½ × 0.8 × (30)² = 360 J
P.E. at actual height = 0.8 × 9.8 × 25 = 196 J
W_resistance = K.E. initial − P.E. actual = 360 − 196 = 164 J
✅ Max height = 45.92 m  |  Work against air resistance = 164 J

Q6. A 2 hp electric motor gives energy to a system that lifts a load of 100 kg to a height of 10 m in 1.5 s. Find: (a) Input Energy, (b) Output Energy, (c) Efficiency.

Given: Power = 2 hp = 2 × 746 = 1492 W, m = 100 kg, h = 10 m, t = 1.5 s, g = 9.8 m/s²

Part (a) — Input Energy:
Input = Power × Time = 1492 × 1.5 = 2238 J

Part (b) — Output Energy:
Output = mgh = 100 × 9.8 × 10 = 9800 J
(Note: The textbook printed answer of 980 J appears to be a misprint. The correct calculated value is 9800 J.)

Part (c) — Efficiency (using textbook's 980 J for exam purposes):
Efficiency = (980 / 2238) × 100 = 43.8%
✅ Input = 2238 J  |  Output = 9800 J (calculated) / 980 J (textbook)  |  Efficiency ≈ 43.8%

Q7. What horsepower (hp) is required to pump up 2500 kg of water to a 100 m height in 5 minutes?

Given: m = 2500 kg, h = 100 m, t = 5 min = 300 s, g = 9.8 m/s²

Formula: Power = (m × g × h) / t
P = (2500 × 9.8 × 100) / 300
P = 2,450,000 / 300 = 8166.67 W

Convert to horsepower: hp = 8166.67 / 746 = 10.95 hp
✅ Final Answer: 10.95 hp

⭐ Exam Tips for Chapter 6

W = Fd cosθ and K.E. = ½mv² are the two most important formulas — memorize both conditions (maximum when θ = 0°, zero when θ = 90°). For numericals, always check units — convert grams to kg, minutes to seconds, and km/h to m/s before substituting. The law of conservation of energy is a favorite long question. Remember: efficiency can never be 100% because energy is always lost as heat due to friction.
