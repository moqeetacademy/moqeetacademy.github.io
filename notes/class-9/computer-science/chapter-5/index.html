Class 9 Computer Science Chapter 5 Notes — Applications of Computer Science NBF Syllabus

Unit Overview: This unit explores how contemporary computing architectures drive real-world transformation. Students break down the practical mechanisms of Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning paradigms, Computer Vision, and Natural Language Processing. The curriculum also investigates the cost and accessibility profiles of modern cloud deployments, automated robotics, and critical ethical considerations.

Key Topics Covered

  • Supervised vs. Unsupervised Machine Learning Frameworks
  • Computer Vision and Natural Language Processing (NLP) Tools
  • Automated Systems, Chatbots, and Robotic Engineering Dimensions
  • Cloud Infrastructure Paradigms: Public vs. Private Deployment Architectures
  • Algorithmic Critiques: Bias, Data Quality, and Overfitting Failures
  • Ethical Violations: Deepfakes, Privacy Tracking, and Misinformation Risks

Multiple Choice Questions

1. The process of taking a known data set as input to train the algorithm to create a prediction model is known as:
  • A. Natural Language Processing
  • B. Expert System
  • C. Supervised Machine Learning
  • D. Unsupervised Machine Learning
Correct Answer: C. Supervised Machine Learning
Explanation: Supervised learning uses labeled inputs and targets to map predictive functions for automated estimation[cite: 1].
2. The AI technology that enables computer systems to obtain meaningful information from digital images or videos is called:
  • A. Image Recognition
  • B. Speech Recognition
  • C. Scanner
  • D. Computer Vision
Correct Answer: D. Computer Vision
Explanation: Computer Vision processes raw digital imagery and video frames to extract contextual understanding and descriptive metadata[cite: 1].
3. Instant answers to website visitors' questions through text or voice interaction are provided by:
  • A. Robot
  • B. Chatbot
  • C. Cloud Computing
  • D. Expert System
Correct Answer: B. Chatbot
Explanation: Chatbots simulate conversational interfaces, providing text or speech interactions to answer user questions on websites[cite: 1].
4. Cloud computing model in which resources are owned by and used inside a single organization is called:
  • A. Private Cloud
  • B. Public Cloud
  • C. Hybrid Cloud
  • D. Remote Cloud
Correct Answer: A. Private Cloud
Explanation: Private cloud models isolate hardware and compute resources for the exclusive use of a single organization[cite: 1].
5. The AI tool used for correcting spelling and grammar mistakes is:
  • A. Chat GPT
  • B. Lovo AI
  • C. Grammarly
  • D. Virtual Assistant
Correct Answer: C. Grammarly
Explanation: Grammarly is a dedicated writing assistant designed to fix grammar, spelling, and structural syntax errors[cite: 1].
6. Virtual assistant that belongs to Apple Inc. is:
  • A. Siri
  • B. Alexa
  • C. Google Assistant
  • D. Cortana
Correct Answer: A. Siri
Explanation: Siri is Apple Inc.'s built-in voice-controlled virtual assistant software environment[cite: 1].
7. The AI tool that converts text to speech is:
  • A. Chat GPT
  • B. Grammarly
  • C. Alexa
  • D. Lovo AI
Correct Answer: D. Lovo AI
Explanation: Lovo AI is an acoustic synthesis program focused on transforming written text into expressive, realistic spoken audio[cite: 1].
8. The technology used in self-driving cars is called:
  • A. Natural Language Processing
  • B. Chatbot
  • C. Computer Vision
  • D. Virtual Assistant
Correct Answer: C. Computer Vision
Explanation: Autonomous vehicles rely on computer vision arrays to detect obstacles, track lanes, and read traffic signs in real time[cite: 1].
9. The technology concerned with training the computer to understand spoken and written words and to take action is known as:
  • A. Virtual Assistant
  • B. Natural Language Processing
  • C. Language Analysis
  • D. Virtual Training
Correct Answer: B. Natural Language Processing
Explanation: Natural Language Processing (NLP) bridges human language patterns and computer processing to parse and analyze text or speech[cite: 1].
10. The technology concerned with performing specific tasks with little or no human intervention using computer-controlled machines is called:
  • A. Computer Vision
  • B. Computer Intelligence
  • C. Robotics
  • D. Virtual Technology
Correct Answer: C. Robotics
Explanation: Robotics covers the design and execution of programmable, computer-controlled machines that handle specific physical tasks automatically[cite: 1].

Short Questions

Question 1: Briefly describe the positive impact of AI in our daily life.
Artificial Intelligence simplifies daily routines and boosts personal productivity by[cite: 1]:
  • Smart Organization: Voice assistants like Siri and Alexa manage daily schedules, set alerts, and track tasks[cite: 1].
  • Navigation & Media: Engines like Google Maps calculate real-time transit routes, while streaming platforms tailor media recommendations to user preferences[cite: 1].
  • Societal Progress: Medical systems leverage AI models for early illness detection, and digital classrooms offer personalized learning tracks[cite: 1].
Question 2: Differentiate between supervised and unsupervised machine learning.
Evaluation Metric Supervised Machine Learning Unsupervised Machine Learning
Training Dataset Style Uses explicitly labeled data with defined input-output pairs[cite: 1]. Processes unlabeled, raw data streams with no predefined answers[cite: 1].
Core Algorithmic Target Learns mathematical functions to predict targets for new entries[cite: 1]. Explores hidden structures, common patterns, or groupings within data[cite: 1].
Practical Use Case Estimating real estate prices based on historical property features[cite: 1]. Clustering consumer profiles for targeted market segmentation[cite: 1].
Question 3: Describe how computer vision applications help in the automation of tasks.
Computer vision applications automate complex physical tasks by extracting, parsing, and understanding visual data from the environment[cite: 1]. Key examples include:
  • Self-Driving Vehicles: Scanning and reading road signs, lane markers, and moving obstacles to pilot vehicles without a human driver[cite: 1].
  • Security Infrastructures: Running automated facial recognition models to protect sensitive physical access points[cite: 1].
  • Smart Manufacturing: Using high-speed cameras on assembly lines to automatically spot structural flaws or manufacturing defects in products[cite: 1].
Question 4: Compare the use of Natural Language Processing (NLP) with computer languages for interaction with computers.
  • Natural Language Processing (NLP): Allows users to interact with computers using natural, everyday human languages like English[cite: 1]. This conversational model eliminates the need for technical training, making computers highly approachable for everyone[cite: 1].
  • Programming Languages (e.g., Python, Java): Demand precise, structured syntax and logical rules[cite: 1]. Writing code requires specific software development expertise, making it difficult for non-technical users to communicate directly with computing hardware[cite: 1].
Question 5: What is a robot? Mention four areas where robots can replace humans that are not mentioned in this unit.

Robot: A programmable machine built to perform complex physical actions autonomously or with minimal human input[cite: 1].

Four Specialized Fields of Deployment (excluding typical assembly lines):

  1. Deep-Space Exploration: Navigating extreme planetary terrains and collecting scientific samples under conditions dangerous for humans[cite: 1].
  2. Disaster Response Operations: Leading search and rescue efforts inside hazardous environments like collapsed structures or toxic hot zones[cite: 1].
  3. Deep-Sea Oceanography: Mapping underwater trenches and running marine research tasks at depths with extreme water pressure[cite: 1].
  4. Precision Agriculture: Automating crop planting, checking field health with precision sensors, and harvesting produce across large farms[cite: 1].
Question 6: Is the use of the Grammarly AI tool a perfect replacement for manual proofreading?
No, Grammarly AI cannot completely replace a manual human proofreader[cite: 1]. While it quickly catches typos, fixes grammar slips, and improves sentence clarity, it lacks a true understanding of contextual nuance, subtle tonal shifts, creative wordplay, and cultural relevance[cite: 1]. A thorough human review is still essential to ensure writing is accurate, authentic, and fits the intended audience[cite: 1].

Long Questions

Q1. Relate how AI can be beneficial in education and business.

Artificial Intelligence drives efficiency and updates workflows across both schools and modern businesses[cite: 1]:

1. Transforming Educational Paradigms:

  • Personalized Learning Tracks: AI tutoring software adapts to a student's individual pace, serving up custom practice problems and immediate conceptual feedback[cite: 1].
  • Immersive Environments: AI-powered Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR) tools turn complex, abstract ideas into engaging, interactive lessons[cite: 1].
  • Administrative Relief: Automated systems handle grading, log daily attendance, and assist with lesson plans, giving teachers more time to focus on student mentorship[cite: 1].
  • Inclusive Digital Classrooms: Assistive tools like real-time translation, speech-to-text engines, and adaptive keyboards help students with disabilities access learning materials easily[cite: 1].

2. Optimizing Business Infrastructures:

  • Process Automation: AI bots handle routine, repetitive tasks like manual data entry, freeing up teams to focus on core strategic planning[cite: 1].
  • Deeper Customer Insights: Data systems analyze user behaviors and web trends to help businesses create highly effective, personalized marketing campaigns[cite: 1].
  • Predictive Supply Chains: Predictive analytics anticipate shifting market demands, allowing companies to balance inventory levels and lower overhead costs[cite: 1].
  • Data-Backed Executive Choices: High-speed processing models parse massive datasets, giving executives clear, actionable trends to guide major business decisions[cite: 1].
Q2. Articulate how AI-based technology can improve healthcare professionals and assist farmers increase crop production.

AI technologies improve accuracy and productivity in both clinical medicine and rural agriculture[cite: 1]:

1. Empowering Healthcare Professionals:

  • Early Disease Catching: Deep neural models scan medical imaging (like CT scans and MRIs) to spot indicators of conditions like cancer ahead of traditional timelines[cite: 1].
  • Epidemiologic Forecasting: Predictive tools analyze real-time public data to flag coming virus outbreaks so health centers can prepare early[cite: 1].
  • Precision Surgeries: Surgeons use robotic arms guided by AI to complete delicate, high-risk procedures safely[cite: 1].
  • Continuous Wearable Vitals: Smart wearable trackers monitor patient metrics like blood glucose levels or heart rhythms around the clock, alerting clinics to sudden shifts[cite: 1].
  • Optimized Health Portals: Automated natural language tools manage electronic medical records, keeping user files accurate and organized[cite: 1].

2. Boosting Agricultural Crop Production:

  • Soil Nutritional Profiling: Precision sensors measure soil chemical levels, tracking nutrient and moisture conditions to recommend the best fertilizers and crop choices[cite: 1].
  • Weather Analysis Models: Metereological AI engines forecast shifting seasonal patterns, helping farmers pick the perfect windows to plant and harvest[cite: 1].
  • Targeted Fields Monitoring: Drones equipped with computer vision scan fields from above to find early signs of pest damage, plant disease, or water stress[cite: 1].
  • Sustainable Resource Savings: Automated delivery loops distribute water and pesticides only where needed, reducing waste while protecting crop yields[cite: 1].
Q3. Categorize the benefits of cloud computing. Contrast between public and private clouds.

Core Benefits of Cloud Deployments: Cloud computing eliminates the need for expensive on-site server hardware by moving storage and processing to internet-based networks[cite: 1]. It offers five main advantages: lower upfront hardware costs, seamless resource scaling on demand, access to data from any connected device, automated backup recovery, and real-time project collaboration[cite: 1].

Architecture Feature Public Cloud Infrastructure Private Cloud Infrastructure
Ownership & Hosting Maintained and run on off-site servers by global third-party tech vendors (e.g., AWS, Google Cloud)[cite: 1]. Owned exclusively by or dedicated entirely to a single organization[cite: 1].
Hardware Sharing Compute, network, and storage arrays are shared dynamically among various corporate clients[cite: 1]. Compute power is completely isolated behind dedicated firewalls, with zero outside hardware sharing[cite: 1].
Financial Profile Highly cost-effective, using a flexible, pay-as-you-go model for general operational tasks[cite: 1]. Requires significant upfront capital investments and dedicated ongoing maintenance teams[cite: 1].
Security & Isolation Lower security isolation makes it less ideal for highly sensitive corporate or government data[cite: 1]. Maximum data privacy, making it perfect for financial records and protected intellectual property[cite: 1].
Q4. Compare between traditional algorithms and AI-based algorithms that use machine learning. Criticize AI-based algorithms for producing wrong results, at times.

Structural Divergence: Traditional computing pathways rely on explicit rules written by software developers; these hardcoded loops follow fixed logic that cannot adapt to new situations or unexpected data[cite: 1]. In contrast, AI and machine learning algorithms don't rely on fixed rules[cite: 1]. Instead, they discover patterns by training on massive datasets, allowing them to handle complex challenges like language processing and image recognition while improving automatically over time[cite: 1].

Critical Flaws and System Weaknesses of AI Models:
Despite their flexibility, machine learning models can fail and produce incorrect or harmful results due to several key factors:

  • Training Dataset Bias: If historical training logs contain human biases, the algorithm will repeat and amplify those unfair prejudices in its predictions[cite: 1].
  • Garbage In, Garbage Out: Incomplete, messy, or low-quality data inputs undermine the model's accuracy, leading to flawed recommendations[cite: 1].
  • The Black Box Problem: Deep learning networks are often highly complex and hard to interpret, making it difficult for engineers to trace how a system reached a wrong conclusion[cite: 1].
  • Model Overfitting: An over-optimized model might memorize training data perfectly but fail to make accurate predictions when given real-world data[cite: 1].
  • Severe Real-World Consequences: Algorithmic errors in critical fields like medical diagnoses or criminal justice can lead to devastating real-world harms and damage public trust in AI technology[cite: 1].
Q5. Interpret the ethical issues in using the following AI tools: a) ChatGPT, b) Lovo AI, c) Virtual Assistants.

As AI tools become more common, they bring serious ethical challenges that impact privacy, security, and public trust[cite: 1]:

a) Generative Conversational Engines (e.g., ChatGPT):

  • Spreading Misinformation: Generative models can confidently create false or misleading content, fabricating facts that look authentic[cite: 1].
  • Data Tracking Risks: Private chats and sensitive entries may be stored or used to train future models without the user's explicit knowledge[cite: 1].
  • Malicious Exploitation: Bad actors can use text generators to create harmful content at scale, such as fake news or deceptive phishing emails[cite: 1].

b) Audio and Voice Synthesis Engines (e.g., Lovo AI):

  • Deepfake Audios: Voice cloning technology can be weaponized to generate realistic deepfakes, raising concerns about identity theft and online fraud[cite: 1].
  • Biometric Consent Violations: A person's unique vocal signature can be copied and used from short audio clips without their permission[cite: 1].

c) Ambient Virtual Assistants (e.g., Siri, Alexa):

  • Constant Background Sourcing: Assistants keep microphones active to catch wake words, collecting personal data that could be compromised if handled poorly[cite: 1].
  • Systemic Bias: Depending on how their underlying models were built, assistants can serve up skewed or biased answers to users[cite: 1].
  • Cognitive Dependency: Over-relying on automated voice assistants can weaken personal problem-solving skills and reduce real-world human interactions[cite: 1].
