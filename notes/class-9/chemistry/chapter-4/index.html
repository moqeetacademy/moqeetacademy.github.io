Short Response Questions

Q(i): Define Group and Period in the periodic table.

Group: The vertical column of elements spanning from top to bottom in the periodic table. Elements inside a matching group show similar outer configurations and chemical traits.
Period: The horizontal row of elements moving left to right across the periodic table.

Q(ii): State Modern Periodic Law.

The Modern Periodic Law declares that the chemical and physical properties exhibited by elements are periodic functions tracking their increasing atomic numbers.

Q(iii): Why are noble gases unreactive?

Noble gases possess completely filled valence shell electronic configurations (holding an octet of 8 electrons, or a duplet of 2 in Helium). Because their outermost shells are entirely stable and full, they lack any chemical drive to gain, lose, or share electrons, making them highly unreactive.

Long Response Questions

Q7: The electronic configuration of some elements is given below. Group the elements in pairs that represent similar chemical properties.

Given Data configurations: A = 1s² 2s², B = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶, C = 1s² 2s² 2p³, E = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p³, H = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶.

Answer & Methodology: Elements with similar outer valence configurations share matching columns and display similar chemical properties. Evaluating the given list reveals two explicit pairs:

  • Pair 1: C & E — Both elements end in a p³ valence configuration (C has 5 outer electrons in shell 2; E has 5 outer electrons in shell 3). This places both in Group VA (Group 15).
  • Pair 2: B & H — Both represent stable Noble Gas configurations with completely filled outermost shells ending in a stable p⁶ layout. This places them in Group VIIIA (Group 18).

Q8: For normal elements, the number of valence electrons = group number (in A-group notation). Find the group number for the following elements.

1. Aluminium (1327Al)
Configuration: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p¹
Valence Electrons: $2 + 1 = 3$ (in the 3rd shell).
Group Position: IIIA (Group 13)

2. Element M (Atomic Number = 14)
Configuration: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p²
Valence Electrons: $2 + 2 = 4$ (in the 3rd shell).
Group Position: IVA (Group 14)

⭐ Exam Tips for Chapter 4

A classic conceptual board question involves pairing elements that exhibit identical properties based on their electron configurations. Always inspect the highest energy tier's outer numbers. If two configurations conclude with matching counts (like 2s² 2p³ and 3s² 3p³), they are automatically part of the same group and display matching chemical behavior.
