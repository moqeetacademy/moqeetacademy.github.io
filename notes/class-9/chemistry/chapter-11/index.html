Short Response Questions

(i) List two main sources of acid rain.

The two main sources of acid rain are:

  • Sulphur dioxide ($SO_2$): Released primarily from power stations and heavy industries that burn coal or petroleum products.
  • Oxides of nitrogen ($NO_x$): Released from high-temperature combustion in motor vehicles and industrial manufacturing units.

(ii) List four human activities which contribute to air pollution.

Four primary human activities that drive air contamination include:

  1. Burning fossil fuels (such as coal, petroleum, and natural gas) for energy and transit.
  2. Industrial factory emissions from processing chemical raw materials.
  3. Vehicle exhaust fumes from daily transportation.
  4. Deforestation and large-scale clearing or burning of forest land.

(iii) What is the importance of catalytic converters?

Catalytic converters are critical automotive safety systems attached to vehicle exhausts. They lower air pollution by chemically converting toxic gases (such as carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, and unburnt hydrocarbons) into safer substances like carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), nitrogen gas ($N_2$), and water vapor ($H_2O$) before they enter the atmosphere.

Long Response Questions

Q4. Describe how acid rain is produced. Write balanced chemical equations to support your answer.

Acid rain is formed when acidic primary pollutants like sulphur dioxide ($SO_2$) and nitrogen dioxide ($NO_2$) are emitted into the atmosphere by factories and vehicles. These gases rise up and react with atmospheric water vapor, oxygen, and other chemicals to form weak solutions of sulphuric acid and nitric acid. When precipitation occurs, these chemicals fall to the ground as acid rain.

Balanced Chemical Equations:

1. Formation of Sulphuric Acid:
$$2SO_2(g) + O_2(g) + 2H_2O(l) \rightarrow 2H_2SO_4(aq)$$

2. Formation of Nitric Acid:
$$4NO_2(g) + O_2(g) + 2H_2O(l) \rightarrow 4HNO_3(aq)$$

Q5. Explain global warming. How does it relate to the greenhouse effect?

Global warming refers to the steady rise in the average temperature of the Earth's atmosphere and oceans, driven by human activities. It is caused by the increase of greenhouse gases—such as carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), methane ($CH_4$), and water vapor ($H_2O$)—released by burning fossil fuels, industrial processing, and deforestation.

These accumulated gases create a dense layer in the upper atmosphere. They allow incoming short-wave solar radiation to pass through to the Earth's surface but trap the outgoing long-wave infrared thermal radiation reflected from the ground. This heat-retention mechanism mimics a greenhouse, warming the planet and altering global climate patterns.

Q6. What is ground level ozone? Explain.

Ground-level ozone ($O_3$) is a hazardous secondary air pollutant formed near the Earth's surface. Unlike the beneficial stratospheric ozone layer that shields us from ultraviolet radiation, ground-level ozone is created when volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$) undergo photochemical reactions in the presence of sunlight.

It forms a major component of urban photochemical smog. Breathing ground-level ozone causes respiratory difficulties, triggers asthma, reduces lung function, and damages vegetation and agricultural crops.

Q7. Why is global warming often referred to as the greenhouse effect? Justify your answer.

Global warming is called the greenhouse effect because the underlying atmospheric heat-retention process works just like a structural glass greenhouse used for cultivating plants in cold weather.

In a greenhouse, the glass panels allow sunlight to enter but prevent the accumulated heat from escaping, warming the interior. Similarly, Earth's greenhouse gases ($CO_2$, $CH_4$, $H_2O$) act like glass panels. They let solar radiation reach the planet's surface but trap the reflected thermal energy, raising the overall temperature of the Earth.

Q8. Sulphur dioxide is a common pollutant from burning coal. State two effects caused by this pollutant.

Sulphur dioxide ($SO_2$) emissions cause several severe environmental and health issues, including:

  • Respiratory Health Problems: It irritates the human respiratory tract, causing severe breathing difficulties, throat irritation, acute coughing, and chronic conditions like asthma and bronchitis.
  • Acid Rain Formation: It reacts with atmospheric moisture to form sulphuric acid, creating acid rain that lowers the pH of water bodies, harms aquatic life, degrades soil quality, and corrodes architectural structures.

⭐ Exam Tips for Chapter 11

Be careful to distinguish between primary and secondary pollutants. Primary pollutants (like $SO_2$ or $NO_x$) are emitted directly from sources, while secondary pollutants (like acid rain or ground-level ozone) form through chemical reactions in the atmosphere. Remember to write out complete, balanced equations when discussing acid rain formation on your exams!
