Short Response Questions

Q(i): Why do atoms form chemical bonds?

Atoms form chemical bonds to achieve stability. They do this by gaining, losing, or sharing electrons to complete their outermost valence shell, acquiring the stable electronic configuration of the nearest noble gas (octet or duplet).

Q(ii): State the Octet rule.

Octet Rule: The tendency of atoms to gain, lose, or share electrons in order to acquire a total of eight electrons in their outermost valence shell to achieve chemical stability.

Long Response Questions

Q5. Briefly describe three main types of chemical bonds.

The three principal types of chemical bonds are:

  • Ionic Bond: Formed by the complete transfer of electrons from a metallic atom to a non-metallic atom (e.g., NaCl).
  • Covalent Bond: Formed by the mutual sharing of valence electrons between non-metallic atoms (e.g., $Cl_2$).
  • Metallic Bond: Formed due to the mutual attraction between metallic cations and a mobile sea of free electrons surrounding them.

Each bonding mechanism allows participating atoms to complete their valence configurations to reach a stable state.

Q6. Describe the formation of a covalent bond between two non-metallic elements.

Non-metallic elements have high ionization energies and tend to share electrons rather than transfer them. When two non-metals interact, they share pairs of valence electrons to complete their outer shells.

Example: In a chlorine molecule ($Cl_2$), each chlorine atom possesses 7 valence electrons and requires 1 more. Both share one electron mutually, creating a single covalent bond ($Cl-Cl$), allowing both to successfully attain a stable octet configuration.

Q7. Explain with examples, single, double and triple covalent bonds.

  • Single Covalent Bond: Formed when one pair of electrons is shared between two atoms. It is represented by a single line (—). Example: Hydrogen gas ($H_2$, $H-H$), where each atom shares 1 electron to satisfy its duplet.
  • Double Covalent Bond: Formed when two pairs of electrons are shared between two atoms. It is represented by a double line (=). Example: Oxygen gas ($O_2$, $O=O$), where each oxygen atom shares 2 electrons to complete its octet.
  • Triple Covalent Bond: Formed when three pairs of electrons are shared between two atoms. It is represented by three lines (≡). Example: Nitrogen gas ($N_2$, $N≡N$), where each nitrogen atom shares 3 electrons to attain structural stability.
