Class 9 Chemistry — Chapter 17: Separation of Mixtures Notes

Syllabus: National Book Foundation (NBF) / FBISE & Punjab Boards | Academic Year: 2026

Chapter Overview: Chapter 17 covers the techniques used to separate mixtures into their pure components. Students learn about filtration, evaporation, distillation, fractional distillation, and other separation methods based on the physical properties of substances.

Key Topics in This Chapter

  • Definition of mixtures and why separation is important
  • Filtration: separating insoluble solids from liquids
  • Evaporation: recovering dissolved solids from solutions
  • Distillation: separating miscible liquids by boiling point
  • Fractional distillation for liquids with close boiling points
  • Magnetic separation, differential solubility, and sublimation

Short Answer Questions

Q(i). Why is it important to separate materials from a mixture?

Answer: It is highly important to separate materials from a mixture so that useful and pure substances can be obtained for practical purposes. Separation processes help effectively eliminate harmful contaminants, improve overall product quality, and make basic elements or chemicals safe for use in daily life, specialized industries, and modern pharmaceuticals.

Q(ii). What is distillation?

Answer: Distillation is a sophisticated separation technique used to divide the various components of a liquid mixture based on their unique, differing boiling points. During this process, the liquid constituent with the lowest boiling point evaporates first into a vapor stream and is then systematically condensed back into its liquid form via a cooling mechanism to be collected separately.

Q(iii). What is filtration?

Answer: Filtration is a structural mechanical method used to isolate an insoluble solid particle substance from a liquid medium. The heterogenous mixture is passed through a porous barrier material, such as filter paper or a membrane, which easily allows the fluid phase to drain through while trapping the solid residue on the surface of the filter filter structure.

Q(iv). What is evaporation?

Answer: Evaporation is a thermal separation method used to isolate a dissolved solid solute from its liquid solvent matrix by heating the solution. As thermal energy increases, the liquid component undergoes a phase transition into a gaseous vapor, leaving behind the pure, solid crystalline material at the bottom of the vessel.

Long Answer Questions / Textbook Exercises

Q2. How will you separate a mixture of sand and water?

Answer: A mixture of insoluble sand grains and water can be successfully isolated using physical separation techniques:

  • Filtration Method: This is the most efficient lab approach. The sandy slurry is poured carefully over a conical filter paper setup fixed within a funnel. The water molecules pass smoothly through the porous paper networks as the filtrate, while the larger sand particles remain trapped on the top surface as solid residue.
  • Sedimentation and Decantation Method: Alternatively, the mixture can be left undisturbed for an extended period. Because sand has a higher density, gravity causes the particles to settle uniformly at the bottom of the vessel (sedimentation). Once settled, the clear top layer of water is carefully poured off into a clean secondary container without disturbing the sediment bed (decantation).

Q3. What technique would you use to separate alcohol from water?

Answer: To successfully separate a miscible solution of alcohol and water, the process of Distillation must be utilized.

This technique relies directly on the distinct gap between the boiling points of the two liquids (pure ethanol boils at approximately 78.3°C, whereas water boils at 100°C). When thermal energy is applied evenly to the solution, the alcohol evaporates at a faster rate, transforming into a vapor column. These vapors are funneled through a cooling condenser jacket, where they transition back into a liquid state and drop into a dedicated collection flask as a high-purity distillate.

Q4. What are mixtures?

Answer: Mixtures are physical combinations comprising two or more distinct substances that are blended together without undergoing any form of chemical bonding or molecular reaction.

Crucially, because no chemical synthesis occurs, the individual component substances retain their original fundamental properties (such as color, solubility, and melting point). Because they are held together strictly by physical interactions, mixtures can be readily separated back into their pure baseline forms using physical methods like filtration, evaporation, crystallization, or distillation.

Q5. How separation of mixtures of two or more liquids is done? Name the separation technique.

Answer: The separation of a complex mixture containing two or more miscible liquid components is achieved using the technique called Fractional Distillation.

This advanced procedure works by exploiting small, progressive differences in the boiling points of the mixed liquids. The apparatus includes a specialized fractionating column packed with glass beads or internal trays. This structural configuration creates multiple cooling and re-evaporation stages as the thermal vapors rise. This enables highly efficient separation, allowing each liquid to be isolated with great purity as its specific boiling threshold is reached sequentially.

Q6. How to separate a mixture of two solids?

Answer: A heterogeneous dry mixture of two solid substances can be cleanly separated by analyzing the physical and chemical differences between the two solids. Common methods include:

  • Differential Solubility: Finding a solvent (like water or alcohol) that dissolves only one of the solids, leaving the other intact. This allows for simple filtration followed by evaporation.
  • Sublimation: Used if one of the solids transforms directly from a solid phase into a gas when heated (e.g., ammonium chloride or iodine), bypassing the liquid phase completely.
  • Magnetic Separation: Utilized if one solid possesses strong ferromagnetic properties (like iron filings) while the other does not (like sulfur or sand dust).
  • Manual Sorting / Sieving: Simple physical sorting using mechanical screens based strictly on grain size variations or visible color differences.

Q7. What method will you use to separate a sand and sugar mixture?

Answer: To separate a dry solid mixture of sand and sugar, a multi-stage process leveraging Differential Solubility must be carried out as follows:

  1. Dissolution Stage: Add an adequate volume of distilled water to the mixed solids and stir thoroughly. The crystalline sugar is highly soluble and dissolves completely into the solvent matrix, forming a liquid sugar solution. The sand remains fully insoluble.
  2. Filtration Stage: Pour the resulting mixture through a funnel lined with filter paper. The sand particles are caught on the paper surface as residue, while the clear sugar solution drains completely into the beaker below as the filtrate. Wash the sand residue slightly with clean water and dry it.
  3. Evaporation Stage: Gently apply heat to the collected sugar filtrate solution. The water will evaporate away into steam, leaving behind pure, dry white sugar crystals at the bottom of the evaporating dish.

Exam Tips for Chapter 17

In Separation of Mixtures, expect to draw or label diagrams of distillation and filtration apparatus. Make sure you can clearly explain the principles behind evaporation, crystallization, and sublimation, and know exactly which technique to apply given a specific mixture.
