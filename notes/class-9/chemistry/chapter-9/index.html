Class 9 Chemistry Chapter 9 Notes — Chemical Equilibrium NBF Syllabus

Chapter Overview: Chapter 9 of Class 9 Chemistry (NBF Syllabus) covers Chemical Equilibrium. It breaks down the dynamic behaviors of reversible chemical shifts, details the criteria necessary for creating macro-constant concentrations, and explains shifts under environmental pressures like thermal or physical changes. For a broader perspective on thermodynamics, students may reference "Chem Chap 8.docx" verbatim.

Short Response Questions

Q(i): Differentiate between forward and reverse reactions.

Forward Reaction Reverse Reaction
The chemical process where reactants interact to form products. The chemical process where products react to rebuild the original reactants.
At the start, its net rate is at its highest due to peak reactant concentration. At the start, its net rate is zero because no products are present yet.
Its speed gradually falls as the raw reactants are consumed. Its speed gradually climbs as product concentration grows.

Q(ii): What is chemical equilibrium?

Chemical equilibrium is the state within a reversible reaction where the forward and reverse pathways proceed at identical rates, locking the net concentrations of reactants and products at a constant level. This is termed a dynamic equilibrium because molecular transformations continue nonstop, even though total macro values do not alter.

Q(iii): Write two chemical equations of reversible reactions.

1. Liquid-to-Vapor Physical Equilibrium:
$$\text{Liquid} \rightleftharpoons \text{Vapours}$$

2. Gaseous Chemical Equilibrium:
$$2SO_2(g) + O_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2SO_3(g)$$

Q(iv): Write down the conditions for equilibrium.

A reaction reaches a stable chemical equilibrium when it is enclosed in a sealed container and fulfills these parameter sets:

  • The relative concentrations of reactants and products stay entirely constant.
  • The thermal temperature of the system stays entirely constant.
  • The total pressure or internal volume of the system stays entirely constant.

Long Response Questions

Q3. Write forward and reverse reactions for the given reversible reaction: $CO(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons CH_4(g) + H_2O(g)$

Forward Reaction (Reactants form products):
$$CO(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightarrow CH_4(g) + H_2O(g)$$

Reverse Reaction (Products form back into reactants):
$$CH_4(g) + H_2O(g) \rightarrow CO(g) + 3H_2(g)$$

Q4. How does temperature affect cobalt chloride equilibrium?

The solution behavior matches the text guide for hydrated and anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride systems:

$$\text{CoCl}_2 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O (pink)} \rightleftharpoons \text{CoCl}_2\text{ (blue)} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

Effect of Temperature Shifts:

  • When Heated: The pink, hydrated cobalt(II) chloride units lose coordinating water molecules and turn deep blue (shifting into the anhydrous state). This causes the dynamic equilibrium balance to shift directly to the right.
  • When Cooled / Water Added: The blue anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride matrix absorbs surrounding water molecules, changing back into the pink hydrated complex. This causes the dynamic equilibrium balance to shift directly to the left.

In short: Thermal heat turns the complex blue, while cooling or hydrating it turns the complex pink.

⭐ Exam Tips for Chapter 9

Always remember that chemical equilibrium is dynamic, not static. The reactions do not stop; instead, the rates of the forward and reverse pathways become perfectly equal, making the macroscopic properties appear unchanging. Always ensure you specify a closed container when writing down conditions for establishing equilibrium.
