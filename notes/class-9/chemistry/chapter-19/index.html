Class 9 Chemistry Chapter 19 Notes — Chromatography NBF Syllabus

Syllabus: National Book Foundation (NBF) / FBISE & Punjab Boards | Academic Year: 2026

Chapter Overview: Chapter 19 covers Chromatography, an advanced separation and identification technique used to isolate and analyse the individual components of a mixture. The chapter focuses on paper chromatography, stationary and mobile phases, Rf values, and practical applications.

Key Topics in This Chapter

  • Definition and working principle of chromatography
  • Stationary phase vs mobile phase: roles and differences
  • Paper chromatography: step-by-step procedure
  • Rf (Retention factor) value: definition and formula
  • Locating agents for making colourless spots visible
  • Identifying unknown substances by comparing Rf values

Short Answer Questions

Q(i). Define chromatography.

Answer: Chromatography is an advanced physical separation technique used to divide and isolate the individual chemical components of a complex mixture. The separation relies directly on the differing rates at which the mixture's components partition or distribute themselves between a fixed stationary phase and a moving mobile phase.

Q(ii). Define locating agent.

Answer: A locating agent is a specialized chemical reagent sprayed or applied onto a developed chromatogram strip to make separated colorless chemical fractions visually apparent. It undergoes a specific chemical reaction upon contact with the targeted spots, producing distinct colored markers for identification.

Q(iii). What is $R_f$ value?

Answer: The $R_f$ (Retention factor) value is a dimensionless quantitative ratio used in chromatography to characterize substances. It is calculated by dividing the total linear distance traveled by an individual solute component from the initial baseline by the total distance traveled by the solvent front stream over the same period.

$R_f = \frac{\text{Distance travelled by solute}}{\text{Distance travelled by solvent front}}$

Q(iv). Define paper chromatography.

Answer: Paper chromatography is a specific analytical sub-type of chromatography that utilizes a high-grade cellulose filter paper sheet as the solid stationary matrix. A liquid solvent system (such as an alcohol-water blend) travels up or down this paper sheet via capillary action, functioning as the mobile phase to resolve the sample mixture.

Q(v). What do you mean by paper chromatogram?

Answer: A paper chromatogram is the final visual, physical record obtained at the conclusion of the chromatographic separation process. It consists of the processed filter paper strip showing the isolated, resolved zones or bands corresponding to each individual chemical substance present in the original sample.

Long Answer Questions / Detailed Explanations

Q2. Explain in detail paper chromatography.

Answer: Paper Chromatography is an essential, highly accessible analytical methodology used to resolve complex multi-component mixtures into individual pure fractions.

1. Fundamental Working Principle

The separation depends entirely on the principle of partition. The individual components within the sample display varying levels of solubility in the mobile phase versus affinity for the stationary cellulose water layer trapped within the paper fibers. As the mobile solvent advances, highly soluble components move quickly up the strip, whereas strongly absorbed components trail behind, resulting in clear, separated bands.

2. Step-by-Step Experimental Procedure
  • Sample Application: A horizontal line is lightly marked using a graphite pencil near the bottom edge of a dedicated strip of chromatography filter paper. A tiny droplet spot of the target mixture (e.g., black ink or plant pigments) is applied onto this baseline via a fine capillary tube and allowed to dry completely.
  • Development Stage: The prepared paper strip is vertically suspended inside a sealed glass development tank. Crucially, the bottom edge of the paper is dipped slightly into the chosen liquid solvent (mobile phase), ensuring the sample spot itself remains entirely above the liquid layer to prevent dissolving directly into the reservoir.
  • Capillary Migration: Via capillary action, the solvent slowly ascends the paper fibers. As it flows past the applied sample spot, it dissolves the components and carries them up the strip at different velocities depending on their chemical properties.
  • Termination: Once the solvent front nears the upper margin of the paper, the strip is removed from the chamber. The maximum boundary line reached by the fluid is immediately marked with a pencil, and the strip is thoroughly dried to yield the final paper chromatogram.

Q3. How can you identify an unknown substance by chromatography?

Answer: An unknown chemical compound can be accurately identified using paper chromatography through direct comparative analysis under controlled experimental conditions:

  1. Parallel Running Method: The unknown sample is spotted onto the baseline of the chromatography paper side-by-side with known pure reference standards. The strip is developed within the same tank simultaneously, ensuring all variables remain uniform. If a component spot from the unknown sample travels the exact same distance and mirrors the coloration of a known standard spot, it heavily indicates identity.
  2. $R_f$ Value Comparison: The absolute distance traveled by the unknown substance spot from the baseline is measured alongside the total distance traveled by the solvent front. The $R_f$ value is calculated using the standard formula. Since every pure chemical compound possesses a distinct, characteristic $R_f$ coefficient in a specified solvent system at a fixed temperature, comparing the experimental result against standard reference tables allows for identification.

Q4. Differentiate between stationary and mobile phase.

Answer: The critical operational and structural differences between the stationary phase and mobile phase are outlined below:

Feature / Property Stationary Phase Mobile Phase
Movement Status Remains completely fixed and static in a permanent position during the analytical run. Actively percolates, flows, or moves across or through the stationary bed structure.
Material State (Paper) The solid cellulose structure of the filter paper (specifically the bound moisture film within it). The liquid development solvent system (e.g., distilled water, ethanol, acetone, or blends).
Core Interaction Holds back or retards the movement of sample molecules via adsorption or chemical partition. Dissolves and carries the sample molecules along with its moving fluid stream.
Separation Impact Components with a high affinity for this phase move slowly and stay low on the chromatogram. Components with high solubility in this phase move rapidly and travel higher up the strip.

Exam Tips for Chapter 19

When studying Chromatography, clearly distinguish between the stationary phase and the mobile phase. You must know how to calculate the Rf (Retention factor) value and explain why different components travel at different speeds across the chromatogram.
