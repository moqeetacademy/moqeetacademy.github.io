Short Response Questions

Q(i): Draw the structure of isotopes of carbon.

Carbon possesses three main isotopes: Carbon-12, Carbon-13, and Carbon-14. They all contain 6 protons and 6 electrons but differ in their neutron counts:

  • Carbon-12 (¹²C): 6 Protons, 6 Electrons, 6 Neutrons.
  • Carbon-13 (¹³C): 6 Protons, 6 Electrons, 7 Neutrons.
  • Carbon-14 (¹⁴C): 6 Protons, 6 Electrons, 8 Neutrons.

Q(iii): An atom has 2 electrons in K shell, 8 electrons in L shell, and 3 electrons in M shell. Write down its electronic configuration.

The total number of electrons = $2 + 8 + 3 = 13$ electrons (Aluminium).

Electronic Configuration: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p¹

Q(iv): Write the electronic configuration of 18Ar.

Argon has an atomic number of 18, meaning it holds 18 electrons.

Electronic Configuration: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶

Long Response Questions

Q11. Describe the applications of isotopes in daily life and science.

Isotopes play critical, diverse roles across several domains of modern science and daily life:

  • Medical Field (Diagnosis & Therapy):
    • Cobalt-60 is applied globally to emit radiation aimed at destroying cancerous tumor cells.
    • Iodine-131 is used specifically to diagnose and treat diseases associated with the thyroid gland.
  • Agriculture: Radioactive isotopes function as specialized tracers inside plants to accurately track how nutrients move and are absorbed from fertilizers, assisting in optimizing food crop yields.
  • Industrial Applications: Radioisotopes are deployed inside pipelines to pinpoint underground fluid leaks quickly and safely. They are also widely used in automated factory machinery to scan and regulate uniform thickness layers of paper or sheet plastic.
  • Archeology and Historic Research: Carbon-14 is uniquely valuable for executing carbon dating procedures, helping archeologists accurately determine the age of ancient fossils and organic historical artifacts.

Explanation: Isotopes represent atoms derived from an identical element sharing matching proton counts, but displaying unique neutron totals. While many isotopes remain stable, unstable radioisotopes generate manageable energy emissions highly valued throughout these industries.

Q12. The atomic number of an element is 23, and its mass number is 56.

(a) How many protons and electrons does an atom of this element have?

Answer:

  • Protons = 23 (Since the atomic number directly corresponds to the absolute quantity of protons).
  • Electrons = 23 (Since the atom is electrically neutral, the electron count matches the proton count perfectly).

(b) How many neutrons does this atom have?

Answer:
Neutrons = Mass Number - Atomic Number
Neutrons = 56 - 23 = 33
Therefore, the atom contains 33 neutrons.

Explanation: The mass number aggregates total protons and neutrons contained in a nucleus. Subtracting the baseline atomic number isolates the true neutron quantity.

Q13. The atomic symbol of aluminium is written as 2713Al. What information do you get from it?

From the standard chemical isotopic notation structure of 2713Al, we extract three distinct pieces of structural data:

  • Atomic Number = 13: This denotes that every individual aluminium atom hosts exactly 13 protons in its nucleus, along with 13 structural electrons circulating within its shells.
  • Mass Number = 27: This specifies the collective total sum combining all protons and neutrons bound inside the central core.
  • Number of Neutrons = 14: Derived directly via calculation ($27 - 13 = 14$). This confirms the structural presence of 14 neutrons.
