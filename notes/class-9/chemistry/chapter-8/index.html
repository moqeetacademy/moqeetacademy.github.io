Short Response Questions

Q(i): Define exothermic and endothermic reactions.

Exothermic Reaction: A chemical reaction that releases heat energy to its immediate surroundings. (Example: Combustion processes).

Endothermic Reaction: A chemical reaction that absorbs heat energy from its immediate surroundings. (Example: Decomposition of limestone).

Q(ii): Define enthalpy of a chemical reaction.

The enthalpy of reaction ($\Delta H$) is the defined net quantity of heat energy that is either evolved (released) or absorbed during a complete chemical transformation.

Q(iii): What is anaerobic respiration?

Anaerobic respiration is a specific path of cellular respiration that does not require oxygen molecules to break down glucose and unlock biological energy.

Reaction: $$\text{Glucose} \rightarrow \text{Lactic acid} + \text{Energy}$$

Q(iv): Define activation energy.

Activation energy is the minimum threshold of extra energy that colliding particles must possess, in addition to their basic average kinetic energy, to initiate a successful chemical reaction and yield products.

Q(v): What is the role of a catalyst in a chemical reaction?

A catalyst increases the overall reaction velocity while remaining chemically unchanged at the end of the process. It achieves this by introducing an alternative chemical pathway characterized by a lower activation energy barrier.

Q(vi): Differentiate between aerobic and anaerobic respiration.

Feature Aerobic Respiration Anaerobic Respiration
Oxygen Demand Requires molecular oxygen. Does not require oxygen.
Energy Yield Releases a higher quantity of energy. Releases a lower quantity of energy.

Long Response & Numerical Solutions

Q3. How can you determine the enthalpy of a chemical reaction?

The net enthalpy change ($\Delta H^\circ$) of a chemical system can be determined analytically by summing and comparing individual bond energies. The calculation uses this formula:

$$\Delta H^\circ = \text{Sum of bond energies of reactants} - \text{Sum of bond energies of products}$$

This approach involves adding the raw energy required to break existing chemical bonds in the reactants, then subtracting the energy released as new bonds form within the products.

Q4. Explain how the process of respiration provides us energy.

During cellular respiration, glucose compounds undergo controlled oxidation within the human body. When these glucose molecules are chemically dismantled, stored chemical potential energy is released. The body captures this energy to power vital biological functions, such as physical movement, tissue growth, and standard homeostatic processes.

Q5. Draw a labeled reaction pathway diagram for an exothermic and an endothermic reaction.

The operational distinctions between the two energy states are detailed below:

  • In an exothermic process, the final products hold less chemical energy than the initial reactants, whereas endothermic products hold more energy than their reactants.
  • Exothermic shifts show a negative $\Delta H$ indicating energy release, while endothermic shifts show a positive $\Delta H$ indicating energy absorption.
  • The energy vector points downward for exothermic changes, but points upward for endothermic changes.

Q6. Calculate the enthalpy of reaction between hydrogen and chlorine to form hydrogen chloride from the given bond energies.

Given Bond Energies:
$H-H = 436 \text{ kJ/mol}$
$Cl-Cl = 243 \text{ kJ/mol}$
$H-Cl = 432 \text{ kJ/mol}$

Chemical Reaction: $$H_2 + Cl_2 \rightarrow 2HCl$$

Step 1 — Energy Required for Bond Breaking (Reactants):
$1 \text{ mole of } H-H \text{ bonds} = 436 \text{ kJ}$
$1 \text{ mole of } Cl-Cl \text{ bonds} = 243 \text{ kJ}$
$\text{Total Reactant Energy Input} = 436 + 243 = 679 \text{ kJ}$

Step 2 — Energy Released during Bond Formation (Products):
$2 \text{ moles of } H-Cl \text{ bonds} = 2 \times 432 = 864 \text{ kJ}$

Step 3 — Apply Thermodynamic Formula:

$$\Delta H^\circ = \text{Energy of reactants} - \text{Energy of products}$$

$$\Delta H^\circ = 679 - 864 = -185 \text{ kJ}$$

Final Answer: $\Delta H = -185 \text{ kJ}$. Since the net energy balance carries a negative sign, this is an exothermic reaction.

Q7. Justify the statement that the process of respiration is crucial for us.

Respiration is fundamental to biological survival for the following key reasons:

  • It oxidizes glucose molecules to supply the body with a continuous stream of usable energy.
  • This energy directly sustains vital somatic and metabolic pathways, including physical movement, cellular growth, tissue repair, body temperature maintenance, and the automated operation of internal organs.

Without cellular respiration, biological organisms cannot harvest the energy needed to power survival mechanisms, making this process absolutely essential for life.

⭐ Exam Tips for Chapter 8

When calculating reaction enthalpy ($\Delta H$) from bond energies, remember that it is always Reactants minus Products. This is the opposite of enthalpy of formation problems ($\Delta H_f$) which you will encounter later, where you subtract Reactants from Products.
