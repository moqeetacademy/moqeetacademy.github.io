Short Response Questions

Q(i): How does chemistry help a doctor to know about the chemical nature of medicine?

Chemistry helps doctors understand what a medicine is made of and how it works inside the body. It explains how the chemical in a drug reacts with the body’s cells to cure diseases. Without chemistry, it would be difficult to know which medicine is safe or effective.

Q(ii): In what ways does technological innovation help to understand the development of new materials?

Technology enables scientists and engineers to develop superior materials for everyday use. For example, chemistry with the help of technology has made strong plastics, waterproof fabrics, and lightweight metals. Computers and machines also help test and improve materials quickly.

Q(iii): Differentiate between geochemistry and astrochemistry.

Geochemistry is the study of the Earth’s chemical structure, such as rocks, soil, and water. It helps us understand pollution, minerals, and natural resources.

Astrochemistry is the study of chemicals in space, like gases and dust found in stars, moons, and planets. It helps scientists understand how the universe formed.

Key Difference: Geochemistry = Earth, Astrochemistry = Space.

Q(iv): With the help of an example, correlate the use of science, technology, and engineering.

Let’s take the example of making a car:

  • Science tells us how fuel burns to make energy.
  • Technology uses science to build engines and control systems.
  • Engineering uses tools and machines to make the car.

So, a car is made when all three—science, technology, and engineering—work together seamlessly.

Q(v): With the help of the Venn diagram, compare and contrast organic and inorganic chemistry.

Organic Chemistry deals with compounds of carbon, like carbohydrates, fuels, and plastics. Inorganic Chemistry studies all other elements, like salts, acids, bases, and metals.

Common Point: Both are branches of chemistry and study elements, compounds, reactions, and their uses in daily life.

Q(vi): What are the uses of nuclear chemistry?

Nuclear chemistry is used across many fields:

  • In hospitals: Radiation therapy is used to treat cancer safely.
  • In energy production: Nuclear power plants generate vast electricity.
  • In research and industry: Radioactive isotopes help in identifying diseases, analyzing chemical reactions, and checking material quality.

It is a highly powerful and useful branch of chemistry, but must be managed carefully.

Long Response Questions

Q3: Define chemistry and its interactions with other matter and energy.

The fundamental definition and core parameters are as follows:

Chemistry: The branch of science that studies matter, its composition, structure, properties, and the changes it undergoes.

Chemistry constantly interacts with other forms of matter and energy during chemical processes. For example, in a burning candle, the solid wax (matter) reacts with oxygen gas in the surrounding air to release light and thermal heat (energy).

Q4: Describe the applications of inorganic chemistry and its importance in our daily lives.

Inorganic chemistry forms the bedrock of multiple infrastructure and engineering products[cite: 60, 62]:

  • It is widely used to develop structural materials like glass, cement, ceramics, and pure metals.
  • It helps researchers and scientists analyze the core chemical properties of essential minerals and industrial salts.
  • This branch holds tremendous weight in commercial sectors, modern industrial processes, agricultural science (such as formulating fertilizers), and building construction.

Q5: With the help of a few examples, highlight the relation between science, technology, and engineering.

Science gives us foundational knowledge, technology applies that knowledge in innovative formats, and engineering designs and builds useful final products.

Example (Mobile Phones):

  • Science: Explains how electromagnetic signals move through air.
  • Technology: Designs precise circuits and modern processing components using that data.
  • Engineering: Physically manufactures and assembles the telephone unit utilizing those modern circuits and base materials.

Q6: Evaluate the role of chemistry in environmental science.

Chemistry allows environmental scientists to detect, evaluate, and successfully mitigate industrial pollution. It provides analytical pathways to check for hazardous elements in water lines, atmosphere, and soil structures, helping us create functional techniques to extract them. Additionally, chemical advancements contribute significantly to engineering eco-friendly alternative fuels and biodegradable products to lower human environmental footprints.

Q7: How does geochemistry help us to solve problems such as pollution and climate change?

Geochemistry tracks how natural and human-made chemical chemicals move across Earth’s geographic layers over long cycles. By assessing these movements, it easily identifies toxic run-offs and heavy elements pooling in soils or groundwater reservoirs. Geochemists routinely test greenhouse gas outputs, acid rain compositions, and topsoil contamination levels, supplying key field data needed to combat climate change and localized pollution issues.

Q8: How is organic chemistry applied in medicines, biochemistry, and industrial science?

Organic chemistry serves as a foundational pillar for multiple overlapping disciplines[cite: 73, 74, 75]:

  • Medicines: It drives the discovery and creation of life-saving medical agents, such as prescription antibiotics and pain-relieving compounds.
  • Biochemistry: It details exactly how vital biological processes function on an explicit molecular scale within living organisms.
  • Industrial Science: It is deployed extensively to create modern polymers, fuels, chemical dyes, industrial coatings, and food additives.
