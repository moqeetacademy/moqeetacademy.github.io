Class 9 Chemistry Chapter 10 Notes — Acids, Bases and Salts NBF Syllabus

Chapter Overview: Chapter 10 of Class 9 Chemistry (NBF Syllabus) covers Acids, Bases and Salts. This block presents the foundational Arrhenius model and the Brønsted-Lowry proton exchange theories, details molecular self-ionization behaviors, and charts balanced equations tracking standard neutralization steps with active metals or metallic carbonates. To compare these patterns with early electrochemistry rules, review "Chem Chap 7.docx" verbatim.

Key Topics in This Chapter

  • Defining Arrhenius acids and assessing tracking behaviors in water
  • Applying Brønsted-Lowry proton donor and acceptor pairs
  • Distinguishing relative dissociation parameters for strong and weak bases
  • Writing balanced neutralization profiles yielding ionic salts and water

Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs)

  • b. $H_2CO_3$

  • b. $MgCO_3$

  • b. Contains OH group

  • c. $H_3O^+$

Short Response Questions

Q(i): Write the equation for the self-ionization of water.

When water molecules undergo self-ionization, they establish the following dynamic equilibrium:

$$2H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)$$

Q(ii): Define and give examples of Arrhenius acids.

Definition:

An Arrhenius acid is a substance that ionizes when dissolved in water to increase the concentration of hydrogen ions ($H^+$).

Textbook Examples:

  • Hydrochloric acid: $HCl(g) \xrightarrow{H_2O} H^+(aq) + Cl^-(aq)$
  • Nitric acid: $HNO_3(l) \xrightarrow{H_2O} H^+(aq) + NO_3^-(aq)$
  • Sulphuric acid: $H_2SO_4(l) \xrightarrow{H_2O} 2H^+(aq) + SO_4^{2-}(aq)$

Q(iii): Why does HCl act as a strong acid?

According to the standard guidelines for acid strength, a strong acid is a species that ionizes completely when dissolved in an aqueous environment. Hydrochloric acid dissociates via the following pathway:

$$HCl(g) \xrightarrow{H_2O} H^+(aq) + Cl^-(aq)$$$

Because all (or nearly all) dissolved $HCl$ molecules break apart to yield free $H^+$ ions, it acts as a strong acid.

Q(iv): Why does $NH_3$ act as a Brønsted–Lowry base?

Under the Brønsted–Lowry definitions, a base functions explicitly as a proton ($H^+$) acceptor. When introduced into water, ammonia exhibits this behavior:

$$H_2O + NH_3 \rightleftharpoons NH_4^+ + OH^-$$

Because $NH_3$ directly captures a proton from the surrounding water molecule to generate the ammonium ion ($NH_4^+$), it acts as a Brønsted–Lowry base.

Q(v): Why does ammonia act as a weak base?

A strong base ionizes entirely within an aqueous medium, whereas a weak base ionizes only to a limited extent, generating a lower concentration of hydroxide ($OH^-$) ions. When ammonia dissolves in water:

$$H_2O + NH_3 \rightleftharpoons NH_4^+ + OH^-$$

Only a small fraction of the total dissolved $NH_3$ molecules react to generate $OH^-$ ions. Due to this partial dissociation, ammonia behaves as a weak base.

Long Response & Balanced Equations

Q3. Ammonium hydroxide and nitric acid react and produce ammonium nitrate and water. Write a balanced chemical equation for this neutralization reaction.

This is a standard neutralization shift involving a base and an acid ($\text{Acid} + \text{Base} \rightarrow \text{Salt} + \text{Water}$). Ammonium hydroxide acts as the base, and nitric acid acts as the acid to yield ammonium nitrate ($NH_4NO_3$) and water ($H_2O$):

$$NH_4OH + HNO_3 \rightarrow NH_4NO_3 + H_2O$$

Q4. Provide balanced chemical equations and chemical explanations for the following reactions:

(i) Sulphuric acid + Magnesium hydroxide $\rightarrow$ magnesium sulphate + water
An acidic compound reacts with a basic hydroxide to yield an ionic salt and water via neutralization:

$$H_2SO_4 + Mg(OH)_2 \rightarrow MgSO_4 + 2H_2O$$

(ii) Sulphuric acid + Sodium carbonate $\rightarrow$ sodium sulphate + water + carbon dioxide
Acids react with metal carbonates to produce a salt, water, and gaseous carbon dioxide:

$$H_2SO_4 + Na_2CO_3 \rightarrow Na_2SO_4 + H_2O + CO_2$$

(iii) Hydrochloric acid + Magnesium $\rightarrow$ magnesium chloride + hydrogen
Acids react with active elemental metals to produce a metal chloride salt and hydrogen gas:

$$2HCl + Mg \rightarrow MgCl_2 + H_2$$

Q5. Identify the Brønsted–Lowry acids or bases in the following reactions and explain your choice:

(i) $HNO_3 + H_2O \rightarrow H_3O^+ + NO_3^-$
Explanation: $HNO_3$ donates an $H^+$ ion to water, so it is the acid. $H_2O$ accepts that $H^+$ ion to yield $H_3O^+$, so it is the base.

(ii) $NH_3 + HNO_3 \rightarrow NH_4NO_3$
Explanation: $NH_3$ accepts a proton to become ammonium, making it the base. $HNO_3$ releases its proton, making it the acid.

Q6. Give the Brønsted–Lowry definition of an acid. Write an equation that illustrates the definition.

A Brønsted–Lowry acid is defined as any substance that donates a proton ($H^+$ ion) during a chemical transformation. An acid must possess a hydrogen atom that it can release as a proton. Upon donating this proton, the acid transforms into its conjugate base.

Illustrative Equation:

$$HCl + H_2O \rightarrow H_3O^+ + Cl^-$$

Q7. Identify the Brønsted acids and Brønsted bases in the following reactions. Classify water as a proton donor or proton acceptor:

  • (i) $CH_3COOH_{(aq)} + H_2O_{(l)} \rightarrow CH_3COO^-_{(aq)} + H_3O^+_{(aq)}$
    $CH_3COOH$ donates $H^+$ ($\text{acid}$), while $H_2O$ accepts $H^+$ ($\text{base}$). Water acts as a proton acceptor.
  • (ii) $HCO_3^- + H_2O \rightarrow CO_3^{2-} + H_3O^+$
    $HCO_3^-$ donates $H^+$ ($\text{acid}$), while $H_2O$ accepts $H^+$ ($\text{base}$). Water acts as a proton acceptor.
  • (iii) $NH_3 + H_2O \rightarrow NH_4^+ + OH^-$
    $NH_3$ accepts a proton ($\text{base}$), while $H_2O$ donates a proton ($\text{acid}$). Water acts as a proton donor.
  • (iv) $HCl + ... \rightarrow H_2CO_3 + Cl^-$ (Note: text matches $HCl + HCO_3^- \rightarrow H_2CO_3 + Cl^-$)
    $HCl$ donates $H^+$ ($\text{acid}$), and $HCO_3^-$ accepts $H^+$ ($\text{base}$). Water is not involved in this chemical transformation.
  • (v) $HS^- + H_2O \rightarrow S^{2-} + H_3O^+$
    $HS^-$ donates $H^+$ ($\text{acid}$), while $H_2O$ accepts $H^+$ ($\text{base}$). Water acts as a proton acceptor.

Q8. Write equations showing the ionization of the following as Arrhenius acids:

According to the Arrhenius model, an acid must generate $H^+$ ions when dissolved in water, splitting into hydrogen ions and corresponding anions:

(a) Ionization of $HI_{(aq)}$:
$$HI_{(aq)} \xrightarrow{H_2O} H^+_{(aq)} + I^-_{(aq)}$$
When hydroiodic acid ($HI$) dissolves in water, it ionizes completely to release $H^+$ ions, behaving as an Arrhenius acid.

(b) Ionization of $HNO_2$:
$$HNO_2 \xrightarrow{H_2O} H^+ + NO_2^-$$
Nitrous acid ($HNO_2$) dissociates within an aqueous solution to produce $H^+$ ions and nitrite ions ($NO_2^-$), fulfilling the criteria for an Arrhenius acid.

⭐ Exam Tips for Chapter 10

Be careful not to confuse the theories: Arrhenius requires an aqueous ($H_2O$) environment to produce $H^+$ or $OH^-$ ions. The Brønsted-Lowry concept is broader and focuses entirely on proton ($H^+$) transfer, allowing substances like ammonia ($NH_3$) to be classified as bases because they can accept a proton, even if they don't contain an OH group.
