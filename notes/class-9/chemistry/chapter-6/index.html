Class 9 Chemistry Chapter 6 Notes — Stoichiometry NBF Syllabus

Chapter Overview: Chapter 6 of Class 9 Chemistry (NBF Syllabus) introduces Stoichiometry. This numeric-heavy chapter covers how to calculate the formula/molecular mass of chemical entities, calculate chemical quantities using mass-to-mole relationships, employ Avogadro's constant ($6.022 \times 10^{23}$), and balance chemical reactions systematically. These detailed textbook numerical exercise solutions ensure complete accuracy for scoring high in your FBISE or Punjab Board final exams.

Numerical Exercise Solutions

Q14. Calculate the mass in grams of the following chemical quantities:

Formula Used: $\text{Mass (g)} = \frac{\text{Number of Particles}}{\text{Avogadro's Number } (6.022 \times 10^{23})} \times \text{Molar Mass}$

(a) $3.24 \times 10^{18}$ atoms of iron (Fe) [Molar mass = 56 g/mol]

$$\text{Mass} = \frac{3.24 \times 10^{18}}{6.022 \times 10^{23}} \times 56 = 3.014 \times 10^{-4} \text{ grams}$$

(b) $2 \times 10^{10}$ molecules of nitrogen gas ($N_2$) [Molar mass = 28 g/mol]

$$\text{Mass} = \frac{2 \times 10^{10}}{6.022 \times 10^{23}} \times 28 = 9.3 \times 10^{-12} \text{ grams}$$

(c) $1 \times 10^{25}$ molecules of water ($H_2O$) [Molar mass = 18 g/mol]

$$\text{Mass} = \frac{1 \times 10^{25}}{6.022 \times 10^{23}} \times 18 = 298.83 \text{ grams}$$

(d) $3 \times 10^6$ atoms of aluminium (Al) [Molar mass = 27 g/mol]

$$\text{Mass} = \frac{3 \times 10^6}{6.022 \times 10^{23}} \times 27 = 1.345 \times 10^{-16} \text{ grams}$$

Q15. Balance the following chemical equations:

(a) Unbalanced: $\text{Na} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2$

Balanced Equation: $$2\text{Na} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2$$


(b) Unbalanced: $\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow \text{N}_2 + \text{H}_2$

Balanced Equation: $$2\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow \text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2$$

⭐ Exam Tips for Chapter 6

Stoichiometry numericals can look challenging because of the large scientific notation exponents. Always separate your calculation into two steps: first find the number of moles by dividing by Avogadro's number, then multiply that result by the molar mass to get the final mass in grams. Double-check your atomic masses before adding them together!
